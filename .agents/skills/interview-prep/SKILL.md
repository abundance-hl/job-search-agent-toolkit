# Interview preparation

Read `AGENTS.md`, `private/master-resume.md`, and the relevant folder under `private/jobs/`. The repository root is three directories above this skill folder. Follow [the workflow guide](../../../docs/workflow.md) for source records and private artifact locations.

Research and drafts only. Never contact interviewers, send messages, submit applications, upload documents, or fill live forms. Browser/computer use is read-only. Sample resumes are examples, not facts about this user.

Refresh time-sensitive company facts, role details, and interviewer titles with official sources and available LinkedIn read tools. Attribute claims, save URLs and dates, and separate verified source text from your interpretation. Do not invent company capabilities, partnerships, customers, growth, or the interviewer's interests. If only an older source is available, label its age.

Create `interview-brief.md` in the role folder, marked `Draft — needs your review`. Aim for a brief the user can scan while preparing a 5–7 minute introduction and discussion, unless they ask for more depth. Include:

- The role's likely problems and success measures, with sourced facts separated from hypotheses to test.
- A short positioning statement grounded in the master; preserve transitions into a new function instead of claiming established tenure.
- A few evidence-backed stories. Outline situation, the user's specific contribution, result, and qualifications on any metrics. Leave unknown actions/results as questions; never complete a story with invented facts.
- Material gaps and honest ways to discuss them, including what the user would need to learn or validate.
- A prioritized shortlist of questions about the work, stakeholders, measures of success, and unresolved role requirements.

For scenario practice, ask clarifying discovery questions before prescribing a solution. In technical/commercial scenarios, distinguish a component, an assembly, a complete system, and an integrator's responsibility; do not imply the employer delivers all of them without evidence. Label hypothetical answer structures as hypothetical.

When rehearsing, ask one interview question at a time and give specific feedback on the answer provided. Do not attribute unspoken experience to the user. Save only useful revised talking points and source updates; avoid exporting private conversation transcripts.
